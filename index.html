A Unified Framework for Target Controllability of Complex Networks under Input Constraints

Xiaole Hu, Jie Ding, and Xiang Li

IEEE Transactions on Control of Network Systems (T-CNS)

A unified minimum-cost flow framework for target controllability under input constraints, covering hierarchical targets, control costs, and robustness.

Abstract

In this paper, we mainly investigate three important problems for target controllability of complex networks, with a particular focus on practical input constraints where driver nodes can only be selected from a predefined subset rather than freely across the entire network. Under such constraints, networks are often not structurally controllable. This necessitates minimizing the number of driver nodes while maximizing the number of controllable target nodes, leading to the constrained target controllability problem. Building upon this, we consider two additional related problems: constrained multilevel target controllability, where the target node set is divided into multiple hierarchical subsets, and the objective is to maximize the number of controllable target nodes of each subset in a levelwise order, and constrained minimum-cost target controllability, which focuses on minimizing the total cost of control paths and cycles. To address these problems, we propose a novel unified framework called “minimum-cost flow based target path cover” (MCTP). Simulation results on Erdős–Rényi, scale-free, and real-world networks demonstrate the effectiveness and scalability of the MCTP framework. Additionally, we introduce two new measures to evaluate the robustness of target controllability with MCTP. The versatility of the MCTP framework suggests that it offers a powerful and generalizable tool for analyzing target controllability problems across a wide range of networks.

Target Controllability of Complex Networks Based on Greedy Optimization

Jie Ding, Yue Zhuo, Xiaole Hu, Yan Zhao, Cong Li, and Xiang Li

IEEE Transactions on Control of Network Systems, vol. 12, no. 4, pp. 2630–2641, 2025

An NP-hardness proof and four greedy algorithms for target controllability, with degree- and frequency-based matching strategies evaluated on synthetic and real-world networks.

Read on IEEE Xplore ↗DOI ↗
Abstract

Controlling large complex networks is a common and important problem. In the field of network control, controlling target nodes is more efficient and feasible than controlling all nodes to achieve the desired functionality for a network system. This article revisits the target controllability problem and theoretically proves that it is NP-hard by reducing it from three-dimensional matching. Moreover, four greedy algorithms are proposed. Three of them follow the order of in-degree, out-degree, and degree for maximum matching during iterations, respectively. The fourth algorithm uses an updated matching order based on in-degree as well as frequency, which reflects the number of times the nodes are matched in previous iterations. Simulations on synthetic and real-world networks are carried out, which illustrate the satisfactory performance of the proposed algorithm using an updated matching order. The results presented in this article provide valuable insights into the efficient control of complex networks in real-world applications.
